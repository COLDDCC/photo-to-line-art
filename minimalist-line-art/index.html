A simpler kind of photo-to-line-art effect

The illustrative mode simplifies a photograph before drawing it. It reduces fine texture, merges small tonal patches and removes isolated ink marks. Broad shaded regions receive single lines or crossed strokes, while bright regions remain white. This is a minimalist hatching effect, rather than a single-line drawing or an automatic outline trace.

Why snow mountains make a useful example

A snow-covered mountain against a clear blue sky has distinct shapes: a relatively uniform sky, white snow and dark slopes. The current algorithm looks for blue sky connected to the top of the image and draws it with pale cyan parallel lines. It uses the selected ink color for the subject’s shaded areas. Cloudy skies, blue buildings and blue shadows can confuse that color-based separation.

What to keep and what to leave blank

A minimalist result does not need every crack, leaf or small contrast change. Begin with a photograph that has a strong silhouette and broad light areas. Use the side-by-side view to judge whether the main shape is still recognizable. Adjust contrast if too much of the subject disappears; lower density if the retained regions look heavy. The result is an automatic interpretation and will not make the same decisions as a human illustrator.

Use the controls without losing the simple look

Keep the line width modest and change one setting at a time. Density controls the spacing within each retained region. The foreground ink and paper background can be customized. Illustrative mode uses a fixed pale cyan for detected sky; it does not reproduce all of the original photograph’s colors. White highlights are intentional empty paper, not transparency.

Save the drawing for your own layout

Download a watermark-free PNG, with a longest side up to 2000 pixels. Use your own source photograph or an image you are allowed to transform. The converter does not provide an SVG, a continuous pen path or a ready-to-cut stencil. Handmade works in the artist section are separate from the images generated by this tool.
