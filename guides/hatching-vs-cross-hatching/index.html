Hatching vs Cross-Hatching

Compare parallel lines, crossed strokes and minimalist hatching before choosing a photo-to-line-art style.

Hatching uses one family of lines

Parallel lines can make a picture look light or dark without filling it with grey paint. Lines that are farther apart leave more visible paper. Narrower gaps and heavier strokes add ink. The lines may be diagonal, horizontal or vertical. In a handmade drawing, their placement can also describe the subject’s form. This converter uses fixed direction choices rather than a reconstructed three-dimensional surface.

Cross-hatching adds another direction

Cross-hatching places another set of strokes across the first. The overlap gives dark areas more visual weight. In the detailed mode here, extra strokes are reserved for darker regions; white highlights do not receive the same stack of lines. Start with cross-hatching when you want more visible shading or an engraving-like density, then reduce line width if the output becomes difficult to read.

Minimalist hatching changes the amount of information

Minimalism is a choice about which information stays, not a third line direction. A drawing can use parallel or crossed lines and still be sparse. Our illustrative renderer smooths fine texture, groups tonal regions, leaves bright regions blank and uses pale cyan for a detected clear sky. It aims for broad shapes instead of recording every change in the source photograph.

Choose a mode for the picture you have

For textured stone, a face with clear lighting or an object with strong shade, detailed cross-hatching can retain more of the photograph. For a bright snow scene or a simple high-contrast subject, illustrative hatching may produce clearer areas of white paper. The same settings will not suit every subject. Keep the original visible while trying the two renderers, and inspect the downloaded image at its actual size.

Density, width and contrast are separate controls

Density changes how frequently lines appear across the picture. Width changes the thickness of each stroke. Contrast affects how strongly bright and dark source values separate. Increasing all three can cover the paper quickly. A useful sequence is to choose the mode, find a comfortable density, and only then adjust width and contrast. Keep one setting unchanged while comparing another so you can see what caused the difference.

Line art does not always mean an outline

Photo-to-line-art search results cover several different products: contour extraction, a single continuous path, pencil simulations and hatching. This website focuses on hatching. It does not promise subject-aware contour tracing, a single-line portrait, editable vector paths or closed coloring-book shapes. Those tasks need different processing. Stating the distinction helps you decide whether the downloadable PNG fits your intended project.

Exporting a line-based picture

The artwork is drawn on a raster canvas and downloaded as PNG. The longest side is capped at 2000 pixels, and small source images are not enlarged. A larger pixel count is useful for printing, but it does not make the file a vector. Fine lines can look different after resizing; preview the PNG at the size you will actually display or print. Uploaded photos are processed locally, without a conversion API or an account.
